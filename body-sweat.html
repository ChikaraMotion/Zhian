Sweat & Odor

You were taught your body should smell like nothing — or like vanilla, coconut, a meadow of flowers. Anything but human. But your body is human. It has scent. It has moisture. It has glands that work all day without your permission. This is not failure. This is function.

Why We Sweat

Sweat is thermoregulation — your body's way of cooling itself. When you're hot, when you're moving, when you're stressed, your sweat glands release fluid onto your skin. As it evaporates, it draws heat away. You are not gross. You are a body that knows how to survive summer, exercise, and anxiety.

Two Types of Sweat Glands

Eccrine glands are everywhere — all over your body. They produce a watery, odorless sweat for cooling. These are active from birth.

Apocrine glands are concentrated in your underarms and groin. They become active during puberty. They produce a thicker, milky fluid — and this is the sweat that interacts with bacteria on your skin to create body odor.

If your body odor changed suddenly during puberty, you were not doing anything wrong. Your apocrine glands had simply turned on — exactly when they were supposed to.

Where the Odor Comes From

Sweat itself is odorless when it leaves the gland. The smell happens when bacteria on your skin break down the sweat. That's it. Body odor is not your fault. It's a chemical reaction you cannot control — only manage, if you choose to.

Hormones, stress, diet, and hydration all affect how you smell. Some days you'll notice it more. Some days less. Your scent changes across your cycle. This is not a sign something is wrong.

Vaginal Odor

The vagina has its own scent. It is not supposed to smell like flowers, soap, or nothing. A healthy vaginal scent is musky, slightly sour, sometimes metallic near your period. It changes with your cycle, with exercise, with hydration, with diet.

You do not need scented washes, douches, or deodorizing products for your vulva. Water is enough. These products disrupt the natural balance and can cause the very odor you're trying to prevent.

🌬️ When to check with a doctor

A sudden strong change in vaginal odor — especially a fishy smell — along with discharge changes, itching, or burning. These can signal an infection, and infections are common, treatable, and not shameful.

Deodorant & Antiperspirant

Deodorant masks or neutralizes odor. Antiperspirant reduces sweat itself. Both are optional. You can use them if you want. You can skip them if you don't. Wearing deodorant does not make you more put-together. Skipping it does not make you unhygienic.

If you choose to use deodorant, look for something mild. Strong fragrances can irritate sensitive skin — especially in the underarm area, where the skin is thin. You don't need to punish your body into smelling like a product.

You are not supposed to smell like a flower.
You are supposed to smell like a body — alive, working, changing.
That scent is not a secret to hide. It is proof you are here. 🌙

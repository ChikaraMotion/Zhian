Discharge

Nobody told you this was normal. Nobody said, "One day you'll find something in your underwear that isn't blood, and it will be okay." Instead, you noticed it alone. You wondered if you were dirty. You wondered if something was wrong.

Nothing is wrong. Discharge is not dirt. It is not infection by default. It is the vagina cleaning itself — a self-washing system that produces fluid to carry away old cells and maintain a healthy balance. You are supposed to have it.

How It Changes Across Your Cycle

Discharge is not the same every day. It follows a rhythm tied to your menstrual cycle. Knowing the pattern can help you understand what your body is doing — and when something might be off.

Right after your period: You may have very little discharge, or feel quite dry. This is normal.

Mid-cycle, around ovulation: Discharge increases and becomes clear, wet, slippery, and stretchy — like raw egg white. This is fertile mucus. It can last a few days. You might feel a sudden gush of fluid — that is discharge, not urine. Normal.

After ovulation, before your period: Discharge becomes thicker, creamier, and white or slightly yellow. It may decrease or become thicker still as your period approaches.

When it dries on underwear: It may look yellowish or stiff. That's oxidation — exposure to air — not infection. It can even bleach dark fabric over time. The vagina is naturally acidic. This is normal, not alarming.

What Healthy Discharge Looks Like

Color: clear, white, off-white, or slightly yellow.
Texture: sticky, creamy, or slippery and stretchy — depending on the day.
Amount: varies from person to person. Some have a lot every day. Some have very little. Both are normal.
Scent: mild, musky, slightly sour. Not odorless — but not foul.

Your discharge will not always look exactly the same. It changes with hydration, stress, diet, and where you are in your cycle. This is not a sign that something is wrong. It's a sign that your body is responsive and alive.

About Products

You do not need scented washes, douches, sprays, or "feminine hygiene" products. The vagina cleans itself. These products disrupt its natural balance and can cause irritation or infections. Water is enough. Mild, unscented soap on the external skin only — never inside.

💧 When to check with a doctor

Discharge that is green, grey, or frothy. A strong, fishy odor. Itching, burning, redness, or swelling around the vulva. A texture like cottage cheese (possible yeast infection). These are not shameful. They are medical conditions — common, treatable, and not your fault. You are not dirty. You just need help, and help exists.

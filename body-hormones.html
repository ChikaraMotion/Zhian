Hormones

You've heard it before. "It's just hormones." Said like your feelings aren't real. Said like your tears, your anger, your hunger, your exhaustion are all an overreaction. But hormones are not a dismissal. They are an explanation.

Your feelings are real. Hormones don't invent them — they shape the landscape they move through. Understanding them doesn't make you weaker. It makes you fluent in your own body.

What Hormones Are

Hormones are chemical messengers. They travel through your bloodstream carrying signals between different parts of your body. They tell your ovaries what to do. They tell your uterus what to do. They affect your brain, your skin, your hunger, your sleep, and your mood. They rise and fall in patterns — not randomly, but rhythmically.

The two main hormones in your menstrual cycle are estrogen and progesterone. They don't work against each other. They take turns.

Estrogen

Estrogen rises in the first half of your cycle — from the start of your period until ovulation. It builds the uterine lining. For many, it brings higher energy, clearer skin, a brighter mood, and more mental focus. You might feel capable, social, sharp.

This is not a "fake" version of you. It's you with a certain chemical balance. You get to enjoy it without apologizing for it.

Progesterone

Progesterone rises after ovulation, in the second half of your cycle. Its job is to stabilize the uterine lining. For some, it brings calm. For others, it brings fatigue, lower mood, tearfulness, or irritability. Both responses are normal. Neither is a personal failing.

In the days before your period, both estrogen and progesterone drop sharply. This shift can make you feel flat, emotional, or easily overwhelmed. You are not losing your mind. You are losing the chemical support that was holding things in place, and your brain notices the difference.

Hormones and Skin

If your skin breaks out before your period, it's not because you ate the wrong thing or didn't wash your face. Progesterone can increase oil production. Pores get clogged. Acne appears — often along the jaw and chin. This is hormonal acne, and it is not your fault.

Hormones and Hunger

Your metabolic rate rises slightly after ovulation. Your body is preparing for a possible pregnancy — whether one happens or not. It needs more energy. You feel hungrier. You crave carbohydrates, chocolate, comfort. This is not weakness. It's math. Your body is asking for fuel because it's doing work.

Hormones and Sleep

Progesterone can make you feel sleepier than usual, especially in the days before your period. If you need more rest at certain times of the month — that's not laziness. It's biology. Your body is not trying to inconvenience you. It's asking for what it needs.

🌊 Your feelings are real

When someone says "it's just hormones," they're often trying to dismiss you. But hormones don't make your anger fake. They don't make your sadness silly. They are the weather system your emotions move through — but the emotions themselves are yours, and they matter.

You are not crazy. You are not unstable.
You are a body with seasons, a mind with rhythms.

Weight Fluctuations

Your weight changes before you understand why. One week your jeans fit. The next week they feel tighter — even though you ate the same, moved the same. This is not failure. This is being female.

Monthly cycle — water retention

In the second half of your cycle (after ovulation), progesterone rises. It causes your body to hold onto more water. You can gain 2–5 pounds of water in just a few days. Your breasts may feel fuller. Your belly may feel bloated. Then your period arrives, and the water leaves. This is not fat. It's tide.

Puberty — growing out, then up

Your body is not growing in a straight line. It grows out, then up, then out again. Hips widen. Breasts develop. The number on the scale goes up because bones and tissue are building — not because you're doing anything wrong. Weight gain during puberty is necessary. It's not a problem to fix.

Carbohydrates and salt

Eating pasta, rice, bread, or salty food pulls water into your tissues. The scale jumps the next morning. By the next day (or two days later), it's gone. This is not real weight gain. It's just water saying hello.

Constipation

Hormones slow down digestion before your period. Waste stays in your body longer. The scale shows that extra weight. When things move again, the number drops. Nothing is broken. Your digestive system is just responding to the same monthly rhythm.

🌙 A quiet note

The scale does not know the difference between water, muscle, bone, food, waste, or the phase of your cycle. It only knows gravity. You are not required to believe everything it tells you.

What helps (gentle, not medical advice)

  • Stop weighing daily if it hurts you. Once a week, or once a month, tells you more about your real pattern.
  • Notice where you are in your cycle before you believe the number.
  • Drink water — it actually helps your body release retained water.
  • If your clothes feel tight before your period, wear something stretchy or loose. That is not giving up. That is being kind to a body that is working hard.

If weight changes feel extreme, rapid, or come with exhaustion, hair loss, feeling cold all the time, or a loss of your period — that's worth mentioning to a doctor. Otherwise, most daily and weekly shifts are just your body breathing.

Your body is not a betrayal. The number on the scale is not a grade.

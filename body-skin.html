Skin Changes

Your skin changes before you ever understand why. One morning it's the same. A week later — oil, bumps, color shifts, texture. This is not a sign that something is wrong. It's a sign that something is starting.

Oiliness

Androgens (hormones that rise during puberty) tell your sebaceous glands to produce more sebum — your skin's natural oil. The forehead, nose, and chin (often called the "T-zone") usually get shinier first. This is not dirt. It's chemistry.

Acne

Not because you're dirty. Because pores are learning to regulate. Blackheads, whiteheads, the occasional deep cyst — common and temporary for most. It can feel unfair, especially when it appears right before something important. That's hormonal timing, not a reflection of you.

Darkening of certain areas

The inner thighs, armpits, neck folds, and groin may become darker. This is often hormonal (sometimes related to insulin, especially in PCOS). It is not dirt. It does not mean you're unclean. For many, it fades over time or simply becomes a neutral part of their skin.

Stretch marks

Growth happens faster than skin can always keep up. Thighs, hips, breasts, lower back — places where your body expands. They start red or purple, then fade to silver. Almost every woman has them. They are not damage. They are evidence of expansion.

🌙 A quiet note

Stretch marks need nothing. They are not a flaw. Moisturizing may soothe itching when they're new, but it won't erase them — and they don't need erasing. Your body is not a smoothed-over illustration.

What helps (gentle, not medical advice)

  • Wash with a mild, fragrance-free cleanser — once or twice a day, no scrubbing raw.
  • Avoid touching or picking at your skin — it rarely helps and often leaves marks longer than the pimple would have stayed.
  • If darkening in body folds bothers you or seems to spread, a doctor can check for insulin resistance. But for many, it's simply hormonal and not a health concern.
  • For stretch marks: moisturize if they feel tender. Otherwise, let them be. They are not a sign of weakness.

If acne is painful, deep, or leaving dark scars, a dermatologist or trusted doctor can offer options. You don't have to suffer silently — but you also don't have to wage war on normal puberty skin.

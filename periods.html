Periods

No one told you. Or they told you with a whisper, a hurried pamphlet, a "you'll understand when you're older." And now you're here, with blood in your underwear and questions nobody answers without making it about babies or marriage.

Let's say it plainly: a period is the shedding of the uterine lining. Every month (or so), your body builds a soft bed inside the uterus — not necessarily for a baby, just because that's what it does. When no pregnancy happens, the lining leaves. That's the blood. That's it.

🩸 What's normal?

A cycle can be 21 to 35 days long. Bleeding can last 2 to 7 days. Blood can be bright red, dark brown, or even nearly black. Clots smaller than a coin are common. All of this is normal. You are not broken.

Cramps

Cramps are the uterus contracting to push out the lining. They're not punishment. They're not weakness. They're a muscle doing its work — but sometimes the work is hard.

If your cramps stop you from living — if you can't stand, can't study, can't breathe through them — that's not "just part of being a girl." That deserves attention. A doctor. A conversation. Help.

For ordinary cramps: warmth helps. A hot water bottle on the belly or lower back. Gentle movement if you can. Water. Not because you're weak, but because you're taking care of a body that's working hard.

🌙 A small note on pain

Pain is not a test of character. Enduring it quietly doesn't make you stronger. Asking for help doesn't make you dramatic. Your pain is real because you feel it.

Irregularity

In the first years after your period starts, irregularity is common. Skipping months. Two in one month. Unpredictable. The body is learning its rhythm. It takes time.

Stress, food, sleep, illness, travel — all of these can shift your cycle. You are not a clock.

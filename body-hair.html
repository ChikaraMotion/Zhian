Body Hair

Someone probably made you feel ashamed of it. A comment in the changing room. A look from a relative. An advertisement that said "flawless" like hair was a flaw. But body hair is not a mistake your body made. It belongs exactly where it grows.

Body hair is human. It appears during puberty — often as one of the first signs your body is changing. It is not masculine. It is not unhygienic. It is not a measure of how well you take care of yourself.

How Hair Changes During Puberty

Before puberty, most body hair is fine, light, and barely noticeable. This is called vellus hair. During puberty, hormones cause some of that hair to become darker, thicker, and more visible — especially on the legs, underarms, and vulva area. This is called terminal hair.

Hair also begins to appear in places it wasn't before — around the nipples, on the stomach, on the lower back, on the upper lip, between the eyebrows, on the toes. All of these are normal. Your body is not doing something wrong. It's doing what bodies do.

Where Hair Grows

Legs. Underarms. Arms. Stomach — a fine line from the belly button downward, or scattered hair across the surface. Lower back. Upper lip. Between the eyebrows. Around the nipples — a few dark hairs or fine ones. The vulva — the outer area, including the outer lips and inner thighs. Toes and feet.

Some women have very little body hair. Some have a lot. Some have dark, thick hair. Some have fine, light hair. The pattern, color, and amount are determined mostly by genetics. There is no correct amount.

Hair Is Not Dirt

You have probably been told — directly or indirectly — that a hairy body is an unhygienic body. This is not true. Hair is not dirty. Washing your body keeps you clean, whether hair is present or not. Removing hair does not make you cleaner. It makes you hairless. Those are not the same thing.

🌿 The shaving myth

Shaving does not make hair grow back thicker or darker. This is one of the most repeated myths about body hair — and it's not true. When you shave, you cut the hair at the surface. The blunt tip feels coarser as it grows in, and it may look darker because it hasn't been lightened by sun exposure yet. But the hair itself hasn't changed. You are not making things worse by removing it.

Removal Is Your Choice

You can shave. You can wax. You can thread. You can use depilatory creams. You can do nothing at all. None of these choices makes you more or less feminine. None makes you more or less clean, more or less beautiful, more or less worthy.

If you choose to remove hair, irritation and ingrown hairs are common. They are not a sign you're doing it wrong — just a sign your skin is sensitive, which is normal. If you choose to keep your hair, that is not neglect. It is acceptance.

Comments from others — family, friends, anyone — about your body hair say more about their discomfort than your body. You are allowed to ignore them. You are allowed to set a boundary. You are allowed to make your own choice and let it be final.

Your body is not a problem to fix.
The hair on your skin is not a failure of grooming.
It is proof that your body knows how to grow, how to protect, how to be. 🌙

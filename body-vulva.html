Vulva

You've probably never seen one that looks like yours. Not in a diagram. Not in a textbook. Not anywhere that counts. What you've seen is simplified, airbrushed, or altered — a narrow range presented as the whole picture. Your vulva is not ugly. You just haven't been shown the full range of normal.

What the Vulva Is

The vulva is the external genital area — everything you can see from the outside. It is not the vagina. The vagina is the internal canal. The vulva is the outer structure — the folds, the openings, the visible parts. Most people use the word "vagina" when they mean "vulva." Now you know the difference.

Parts of the Vulva

Mons pubis: The soft, fatty area over the pubic bone, above the rest of the vulva. Pubic hair grows here. It cushions the bone.

Labia majora (outer lips): The outer folds of skin. They may be plump or flat. Hair may grow on them. They may be lighter or darker than the surrounding skin.

Labia minora (inner lips): The inner folds, inside the outer lips. They can be smooth or textured. They can be shorter than the outer lips or longer and extending past them. Both are common. Both are normal.

Clitoris: A small, highly sensitive organ located at the top where the inner lips meet. Only the tip — the glans — is visible. The rest extends internally down both sides, much larger than it appears.

Urethral opening: A small opening below the clitoris where urine exits the body.

Vaginal opening: Located below the urethral opening. This is where menstrual blood and discharge exit the body.

Labia Variety

Inner lips can be short and tucked inside the outer lips. They can be long and extend past the outer lips. They can be asymmetrical — one side longer than the other. They can be smooth along the edge or have small folds and ridges. The color can range from pale pink to deep brown, and they are often darker than the surrounding skin.

Having inner lips that extend past the outer lips is one of the most common shapes. It does not mean you are stretched out, damaged, or abnormal. It means you have a vulva — one of millions of variations, all normal.

The Clitoris

Most of the clitoris is internal. It extends down both sides of the vulva in a wishbone shape, with erectile tissue that fills with blood. The visible tip — the glans — is covered by a small hood of skin where the inner lips meet at the top. The clitoris has thousands of nerve endings. Its only purpose is sensation.

How It Changes

During puberty, the vulva changes. Hair grows on the mons pubis and outer lips. The labia may grow and change shape. Color may darken — this is normal pigmentation, not a sign of anything wrong. The vulva continues to change with age, hormones, and life stages. It is not supposed to look the same at 15 as it does at 25 or 45.
